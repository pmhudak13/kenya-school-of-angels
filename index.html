The origin story

Before there was a school, there was a priest who paid the fees

Father Chris Musyoka is a Franciscan friar vowed to poverty. For years before the School of Angels existed, he did something quietly radical: he personally paid school fees for bright children from rural communities around Kenya who had no other way to get an education, sending them to schools scattered across the country.

But Father Chris had a bigger dream. He wanted to bring those students home, to build a school in his own hometown of Sofia, in the Machakos subcounty of Kenya, where the children of that community could learn together, close to their families.

"Education is the pathway to ending poverty and illiteracy in Kenya." Father Chris Musyoka, Founder

That dream became the School of Angels. What started as a two-classroom idea in 2014 grew into a thriving secondary school, and in its most recent academic year, it ranked #1 in the ward and #3 in the entire Machakos subcounty. Not despite its humble origins. Because of them.

The partnership

From LA to Sofia, Kenya

When Lieutenant Thomas Giandomenico of the LA County Sheriff's Special Enforcement Bureau (SEB) met Paul Hudak, a Wabash College graduate working near Sofia, and saw those first two classrooms, he didn't look away.

He brought the story back to his SEB team in Los Angeles. Since 2014, the SEB has been one of the school's leading sponsors, a partnership between two communities separated by an ocean but united by the same belief: every child deserves a chance.

Dr. Bill Cook has also visited the school and delivered lectures to students, bringing expert knowledge directly to rural Kenya.

The people behind it

Driven by love for these children

Father Chris Musyoka

Founder and director, Sofia, Kenya

A Franciscan friar who spent years paying school fees for rural students out of his own pocket before building them a home. He runs the school day-to-day through FOPCO, his charitable foundation.
